# Direction C. Creative and abstract: the page becomes a pixel world

Read `common.md` first. This brief sets your direction inside it.

## Stance

The boldest of the three. Treat the page as a small pixel world that the cover's flood opens into. Abstract, art-directed and surprising, but still minimal (few colours, a lot of paper, precise cells) and still fast to read for a hiring manager who gives it 30 seconds. The work must stay easy to find and understand through the concept, never hidden behind it. Invent the concept; the examples below are seeds, not specs.

## Section order

Cover, `#workflow` (2nd on the page), `#work`, then the rest.

## Projects (`#work`)

Each product gets its own place in the world: for example a room, a cartridge, a cell-built screen that a flood-fill wipe opens to reveal the demo, or a level the visitor enters. The real demo video must be the hero of each place, large and legible, with its chapters as part of the world's language. The concept may change how the demos are framed and revealed, not whether they can be watched.

## Workflow (`#workflow`)

A world-scale picture of how Tomas works. Seeds: an overworld map where the 5 stages are places and the agents travel between them; an RPG party screen with Claude Code and Codex as party members, their skills as moves, and Tomas as the player who makes the calls; or a pixel machine whose belts and gauges show the loop. Whatever you pick must make the loop (Review returning to Build or Design), the tools and Tomas's decision points readable within 30 seconds.

## Snapping and transitions

Free within the common hard limits. A pixel transition between sections (a dither or flood wipe tied to the snap) is welcome if it never blocks reading or scrolling and shows the final state under reduced motion.
