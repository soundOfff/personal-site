# Direction B. Middle: one signature interaction

Read `common.md` first. This brief sets your direction inside it.

## Stance

Keep the Flood page recognisable, but give it one new interaction that people remember and talk about. The page should feel like a product showcase with a point of view: confident, minimal, a little playful. Everything else stays quiet so that one interaction carries the page.

## Section order

Cover, then `#workflow` or `#work` (your choice, so the workflow is 2nd or 3rd), then the rest. Say in NOTES.md why you chose that order.

## Projects (`#work`)

A pinned demo stage. While the visitor scrolls through `#work`, a large demo stays in place and the three products step through it one at a time: each product is a snap step, and the stage swaps its demo (with a pixel transition, for example a flood-fill or dither wipe on the 20 px grid). Beside or under the stage, the product name, its one-line description and its chapters. The chapter ticks show progress through the loop and are clickable to seek the video. A small index (1 / 3) or three product tabs let a visitor jump directly. Each product links to its detail page.

## Workflow (`#workflow`)

A pixel assembly line. A ticket travels the 5 stations (Frame, Design, Build, Review, Decide). Original pixel sprites for Claude Code and Codex carry and work on it at Design, Build and Review; Tomas's own marker owns Frame and Decide. When Review fails, the ticket visibly loops back to Build (failed checks) or Design (a wrong approach). Hovering or focusing a station shows its tools from `workflow.tools`. It must still be readable as a diagram when frozen (reduced motion shows the whole line with the loop drawn).

## Snapping

`proximity` on the page at section starts, plus snap steps inside the pinned stage (one per product) that follow the common hard limits. The stage must not trap a visitor: Page Down and a fast flick still move through and past it.
