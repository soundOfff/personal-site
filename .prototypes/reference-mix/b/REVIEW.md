# B · Darkroom review

Reviewed independently in Chromium over the supplied CDP connection, at 1440×900 and 390×844 with touch, with normal and reduced motion. The prototype is usable and the cover delivers its central idea. Fix the false empty state and the two readability problems before presenting it as a finished direction.

## Findings, highest impact first

1. **High — The blog claims there are no matches while showing all 11 posts.** On an untouched `#/blog`, `.empty.hidden` is `true` but its computed display is `grid`; the panel reads “Nothing matches yet” and “No posts match ‘ ’” below the real results. The reset button remains visible after resetting, too. Evidence: [desktop](review/desktop-blog-default-empty.png), [mobile](review/mobile-blog-default-empty.png). **Fix:** in `style.css:319`, add `.empty[hidden] { display: none; }` or scope the grid rule to `.empty:not([hidden])`. Keep `renderBlog()`’s existing result-count logic in `app.js:521`.

2. **Medium — Work-page development makes the header temporarily difficult to read.** The saturated, sharply patterned dither fills the area directly behind black titles, metadata and actions. Sift’s metadata disappears into the pattern; later, screenshot text such as “Session keys” competes with the page’s own text. This lasts through a substantial part of the entrance, beyond the route morph. Evidence: [desktop at approximately 300ms](review/transition-plate-work-0300.png), [mobile at 350ms](review/transition-mobile-work-0350.png), [mobile at 850ms](review/transition-mobile-work-0850.png). **Fix:** in `style.css:254–257`, give the text area a substantially stronger page-colored scrim over both image and canvas, or confine the crisp developing layer to the hero media. Keep the 1.5×/12px blurred image tint; the canvas currently bypasses that blur.

3. **Medium — The fixed navigation collides with reading text.** Difference blending changes color but does not separate overlapping letterforms. At ordinary article scroll positions, Search/Menu and the logo sit directly over prose on mobile; desktop journal cards also pass under the nav labels. Evidence: [mobile article/closing boundary](review/mobile-post-closing.png), [desktop journal](review/desktop-blog-default-empty.png). **Fix:** in `index.html` and `style.css:45`, add a separate backing strip below the blended nav on reading/list views, or mask scrolling content beneath its footprint. Keep the nav’s difference treatment over gallery media, and preserve the current 52px mobile height.

4. **Medium — Waiting offscreen plates keep the animation scheduler running.** At mobile first-load rest, Sift/FocusQuote/Catcher had reached level `0`, while Dex/Dana were below the viewport at level `24`. Instrumenting the existing RAF callback counted **60 `frame` executions in one second** with no interaction. Desktop settled at zero. `pixel.js:201` returns `true` for an invisible plate, keeping it in `tasks` and scheduling another frame indefinitely. Evidence: [mobile final first viewport](review/mobile-intro-2200.png); measured levels `[0,0,0,24,24]`. **Fix:** in `pixel.js:116–135` and `Plate.develop()`, park invisible work outside the active task set, resume it from IntersectionObserver, and cancel RAF when no runnable tasks remain. Add an explicit document-visibility pause/resume gate. Actual hidden-tab suspension could not be measured: this headless browser reported `visible` even after another tab was brought forward. Scrolling the entire sheet away does currently stop its tasks; the defect is partially visible sheets and independently offscreen developing plates.

5. **Medium — Keyboard users cannot scrub the plates.** The Sift link is focusable with a visible outline, but ArrowRight leaves `frame` at `0`; there are no focusable screenshot controls. Pointer movement reaches frame `3`, and a real touch swipe reaches frame `1`. Evidence: [keyboard focus](review/desktop-plate-keyboard-focus.png), [pointer frame](review/desktop-scrub-last-frame.png), [touch frame](review/mobile-swiped-plate.png). **Fix:** in `app.js:194` (`bindScrub`), support Left/Right on a focused plate and announce the current frame through a live status. Preserve Enter as the link activation. Alternatively, add separate labeled previous/next buttons outside the anchor.

6. **Medium — Same-page mobile menu navigation loses the keyboard position.** On home, open Menu by keyboard, Tab to Index and press Enter: the page scrolls to the index, but focus falls to `BODY`; the next Tab starts at “Skip to content” instead of the filter controls. Cross-route menu navigation does focus the destination heading. Evidence: measured focus and [next-Tab screenshot](review/mobile-menu-keyboard-reset.png). **Fix:** in `app.js:784–797`, after closing the menu and handling a same-route section link, focus that section’s heading with `tabindex="-1"` and `preventScroll`, as `mount()` already does for cross-route section navigation.

7. **Medium — A malformed hash bypasses the not-found view and throws.** Changing the URL to `#/%` produces the page error **`URI malformed`** and leaves home visible under the invalid URL. Normal unknown routes do work. Evidence: [invalid-hash state](review/malformed-hash.png) and the captured page-error line. **Fix:** in `app.js:657` (`resolve`) catch decoding failure and return `NOT_FOUND`; in `renderNotFound()` at `app.js:636`, display the safely escaped raw hash when decoding fails. Both decoding sites need handling.

8. **Polish — Two hover rules escape the required pointer guard.** `style.css:285` (`.prose a:hover`) and `style.css:315` (`.blog-search .clear:hover`) are outside `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`, contrary to the notes’ claim that all hover rules are guarded. **Fix:** move these two rules into the same guarded media query as the other hover states. Keep focus and press styling independently available.

## Keep

- The cover’s staggered development and reliable replay after fully leaving it. It is recognizable without becoming a full-screen wait.
- The wordmark/staircase, restrained paper-and-cobalt palette, and asymmetric selected-work rows.
- Pointer scrubbing and real touch swipes, with the shared image transition into work detail.
- Search completion and keyboard result navigation; the visible copy feedback; dates and reading times on every post card.
- The complete content/route coverage, useful offsite fallbacks, compact mobile chips, and reduced-motion final states.

## Design judgment

The cover does deliver Tomas’s requested pixel moment: the coarse cobalt plates visibly resolve into his own work, and repeating the sequence on return feels intentional. The strongest version of this direction keeps that photographic reveal but gives reading and project metadata a quiet, consistently legible surface. Better header contrast and keyboard access to the little screenshot discoveries would make it feel delightful as well as visually distinctive.
